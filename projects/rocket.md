# NAR Level 1 Certification Rocket

**Independent Engineering Project | September 2026 – Present**

**Tools:** OpenRocket, SOLIDWORKS

**Status:** Design and Simulation in Progress

## Project Overview

I am independently designing a high-power rocket in preparation for National Association of Rocketry (NAR) Level 1 certification. The project involves modeling the rocket's geometry, analyzing its flight performance and stability, selecting construction materials, and developing a recovery system before construction and launch.

The goal is to design, construct, and successfully recover a rocket powered by an H-class motor while applying engineering principles to evaluate its performance.

## Rocket Design and Configuration

I developed a preliminary rocket configuration using OpenRocket to evaluate its geometry, mass properties, and predicted flight characteristics.

The preliminary design includes:

- **Rocket motor:** AeroTech H100W
- **Overall length:** 37.483 in
- **Maximum body diameter:** 3.13 in
- **Estimated mass with motor:** 30.1 oz
- **Recovery system:** Parachute with shock cords

The planned construction uses cardboard for the body and motor tubes, plywood for structural components, and a 3D-printed PLA nose cone designed using SOLIDWORKS.

These materials were selected based on cost, availability, manufacturability, and the requirements of the rocket design.

## Stability and Flight Performance

I used OpenRocket to simulate the rocket's preliminary flight trajectory and evaluate its stability, maximum altitude, velocity, and recovery performance.

The preliminary simulation produced the following results:

| Parameter | Predicted Value |
|---|---|
| Apogee | 2,737 ft |
| Maximum Velocity | 624 ft/s |
| Maximum Mach Number | 0.560 |
| Static Stability Margin at Mach 0.300 | 1.74 calibers |
| Maximum Acceleration | 457 ft/s² |
| Time to Apogee | 11.3 s |
| Total Flight Time | 270 s |
| Landing Velocity | 13.3 ft/s |

The calculated static stability margin of 1.74 calibers at Mach 0.300 indicates a restoring tendency following small angular disturbances at that flight condition.

The simulation also provides preliminary predictions of the rocket's flight trajectory and recovery performance, which will be useful during design refinement and preparation for launch.

## Recovery System

The rocket will incorporate a parachute recovery system connected through shock cords. The parachute is intended to deploy near apogee using the motor's ejection charge after the programmed delay.

The preliminary simulation predicts a deployment velocity of 7.14 ft/s and a landing velocity of 13.3 ft/s.

These predictions will help evaluate the recovery system before the certification flight.

## Next Steps

The project is currently in the design and simulation stage. The remaining objectives include:

- Finalizing the rocket geometry and material selection.
- Completing the SOLIDWORKS design of the nose cone.
- Fabricating and assembling the rocket components.
- Integrating and checking the recovery system.
- Conducting the NAR Level 1 certification flight.
- Comparing actual flight performance with preliminary simulation results.

Once construction and testing are completed, I plan to update this portfolio with photographs, flight observations, and comparisons between predicted and actual performance.
