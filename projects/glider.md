# Glider Design and Flight Testing

**The University of Alabama | October – December 2023**

**Role:** Team Lead

**Project Type:** Group Aerospace Engineering Design Project

**Methods:** Aircraft Design, Fabrication, Flight Testing, Project Management

## Project Overview

I led a four-person student engineering team in the design, construction, and testing of an unpowered glider for an aerospace engineering competition.

The objective was to develop a lightweight glider capable of maintaining a controlled flight trajectory while satisfying dimensional, weight, material, and budget constraints.

The project involved preliminary design, material selection, construction, flight testing, performance calculations, and technical documentation.

![Completed balsa wood glider](/glider-photo.png)

*Figure 1. Completed glider constructed by our engineering team.*

## Design Requirements

The glider was designed around several constraints:

- Fuselage length and wingspan between 0.5 and 1.5 meters.
- Maximum empty weight below 15 N.
- No propulsion system.
- Hand launch from a stationary position.
- Flight through an arch positioned 5 meters downstream.
- Total project cost below $50.

## Glider Design and Construction

Our team chose to construct the glider rather than purchase one so that we could adjust its geometry and configuration during development.

Balsa wood was selected as the primary structural material because of its low weight and ease of fabrication.

The glider incorporated a main wing, fuselage, horizontal stabilizer, and vertical stabilizer. A payload of four quarters was used, with attention given to weight distribution and center-of-gravity placement.

![Glider design drawings](/glider-design.png)

*Figure 2. Post-build glider configuration, dimensions, and center-of-gravity location.*

The final design had the following characteristics:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Fuselage Length | 91.4 cm |
| Wingspan | 70 cm |
| Wing Area | 579 cm² |
| Wing Aspect Ratio | 8.46 |
| Empty Mass | 72.0 g |
| Payload | 4 quarters |

## Flight Testing and Performance

After construction, our team conducted hand-launch flight tests to evaluate the glider's performance.

Flight conditions and observations were recorded and used to calculate performance characteristics.

![Glider flight-test data](/glider-results1.png)

*Figure 3. Recorded flight-test conditions and measurements.*

![Calculated glider performance](/glider-results2.png)

*Figure 4. Calculated aerodynamic performance, including glide speed, lift-to-drag ratio, glide angle, and wing loading.*

The reported performance calculations included:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Estimated Glide Speed | 6.1 m/s |
| Calculated Glide Angle | 11° |
| Calculated Lift-to-Drag Ratio | 5.14 |
| Wing Loading with Payload | 15.9 N/m² |

These results provided an initial assessment of the glider's aerodynamic performance under the tested conditions.

## Project Management and Budget

As team lead, I helped coordinate the design, construction, testing, and documentation of the project.

Our team used a work breakdown structure (WBS) and Gantt chart to organize responsibilities, track progress, and meet project deadlines.

The project had a maximum budget of $50, and the final documented expenditure was $23.74.

![Glider project Gantt chart](/glider-gantt.png)

## Engineering Takeaways

This project gave me hands-on experience with aircraft design, material selection, fabrication, flight testing, and project management.

It also strengthened my understanding of the relationship between aircraft geometry, center-of-gravity placement, and flight performance.

Working with a team provided experience in coordinating technical responsibilities, managing project constraints, and applying engineering concepts to a physical design.
